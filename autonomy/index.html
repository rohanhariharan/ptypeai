Autonomy

Typecode can keep working after the first turn. Pick a mode, give it a policy, and it writes the next prompt, runs it, and goes again. You decide when it stops.

What it does

  • Three modes

    One turn at a time, a prompt written for you, or a prompt that sends itself.

  • A policy, not a guess

    Six answers set what may be picked up, what counts as done, and how the loop reacts.

  • Failures steer the loop

    A failed check or a blocked task goes into the next prompt, so the work moves toward the goal instead of repeating.

  • A real stop condition

    The loop ends on the completion marker, when the tasks are done, after eight automatic steps, or on a repeated failure.

  • You can still stop it

    Escape twice ends the turn, and the transcript keeps a quiet stopped rule where it happened.

  • Fixed or written

    The next prompt can come from the six scenario prompts in the policy or from a smaller writer model.

Pick a mode

The robot in the prompt bar opens the three modes. Pick one, and the same turn runs again with a prompt written for the next step.

  • Agent loop

    One turn at a time. Typecode answers, files nothing extra, and stops.

  • Semi-autonomous

    Typecode writes the next prompt. You read it and press send.

  • Self prompting

    Typecode writes the prompt and sends it itself, until the policy says stop.

Policy

A policy is six answers. They compile into one system contract that the agent carries for the whole run, plus one fixed prompt for each point in the loop: continue, error, question, complete, checkpoint and stuck.

  • What may start

    The open project, the next task only, or the tasks you have marked ready.

  • Where it stops

    Every task closed, every check passing, or a product that runs end to end. A failed check that crosses your line pauses the loop.

  • When it gets stuck

    Ask and wait, replan once and retry, or close the run with what it learned.
